# AI security in the Microsoft Zero Trust Workshop

In a Zero Trust architecture, AI introduces new risks and considerations across data, identities, applications, and user interactions. Securing AI is not only about protecting the underlying models and services, but also ensuring that how AI systems are accessed, used, and governed aligns with Zero Trust principles. The AI pillar focuses on controlling access to AI systems, protecting sensitive data used by AI, and monitoring AI usage to reduce risk.

AI pillar guidance focuses on governing AI usage, protecting data interactions with AI systems, enforcing access controls, preventing data leakage, and monitoring AI-related activity across the organization.

The AI workshop covers the following implementation areas:

- **Discover and govern AI usage**  
  Identify where AI tools and services are being used across the organization, including approved and unapproved applications. Classify AI applications, establish governance policies, and control which services are allowed and how they can be used.
- **Control access to AI applications and services**  
  Enforce identity- and context-based access to AI systems. Apply Conditional Access, session controls, and application governance policies to ensure that only authorized users on trusted devices can access AI applications.
- **Apply SaaS and application governance to AI tools**  
  Manage AI applications as cloud services by applying app discovery, session controls, and usage restrictions. Enforce policies that govern how users interact with AI applications, including blocking or limiting high-risk scenarios.
- **Protect sensitive data in AI interactions**  
  Ensure that sensitive data is not exposed or misused when submitted to AI systems (prompts) or used for grounding. Apply data classification, labeling, and data loss prevention (DLP) policies to control what data can be accessed or used by AI services.
- **Prevent data leakage and oversharing through AI outputs**  
  Monitor and restrict the sharing of sensitive information generated by AI systems. Apply policies to block or limit risky output scenarios, including copying, exporting, or sharing AI-generated content containing sensitive data.
- **Control AI-generated content and downstream usage**  
  Govern how AI-generated content is stored, shared, and reused. Ensure outputs comply with organizational policies, including handling of sensitive, confidential, or regulated information.
- **Secure AI-integrated applications and workflows**  
  Protect applications that integrate AI capabilities by enforcing proper authentication, authorization, and data access controls. Ensure AI features follow the same Zero Trust principles as other workloads and do not introduce unintended data exposure paths.
- **Protect connected data and grounding sources**  
  Ensure that data accessed by AI systems—such as Microsoft Graph, enterprise data stores, and connected services—is properly secured. Prevent over-permissioned access that could lead to unintended data exposure through AI responses.
- **Monitor AI activity and usage patterns**  
  Gain visibility into how AI tools are being used across the organization. Track usage patterns, detect anomalies, and identify risky behaviors such as excessive data access or unusual interaction patterns.
- **Integrate AI signals into security operations (SecOps)**  
  Incorporate AI-related events, alerts, and data risks into centralized monitoring and response workflows. Correlate AI activity with identity, device, and data signals to detect and respond to potential threats.
- **Establish AI governance and risk management processes**  
  Define policies, roles, and oversight mechanisms for responsible AI use. Address AI-specific risks such as prompt injection, data exposure, and misuse. Continuously assess risks related to AI adoption and update controls as the threat landscape evolves.
